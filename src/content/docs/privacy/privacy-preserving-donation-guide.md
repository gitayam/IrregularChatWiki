# Privacy-Preserving Donation Guide

Donating to causes and organizations you care about is important, but it's equally vital to protect your personal information during the process. This guide provides practical steps to maintain your privacy while supporting the causes you value.

## Why Privacy Matters When Donating

Many donation platforms require personal details like your name, email, and payment information. While most organizations have good intentions, your data may be:

- Stored in databases vulnerable to future breaches or leaks.
- Shared with third parties, including advertisers or data brokers.
- Publicly listed in donation reports or acknowledgments.

By taking steps to isolate your data and ensure anonymity, you can safeguard your privacy against both present and future risks.

## Techniques for Private Donations

### Virtual Cards for Private Payments

Virtual cards allow you to make payments without exposing your actual credit or debit card information. These cards can be used for one-time or recurring donations. They create virtual credit cards which charge a transaction fee to the vendor which is how they make money.

**Recommended Services**:

- [Privacy.com](https://privacy.com) (U.S. only): Generate virtual cards with spending limits and merchant-specific use. (Free Tier Available)
- [MySudo](https://anonyome.com/individuals/mysudo/): Create virtual cards, emails, and phone numbers that stay isolated. (Paid)

**How to Use Virtual Cards for Donations**:

1. Sign up for a virtual card service and link your primary bank account.
2. Generate a virtual card for the specific organization you're donating to.
3. Set spending limits to prevent overcharges or unauthorized transactions.
4. Use the virtual card details to complete your donation.

**Why Virtual Cards Help**:

- Limit exposure of your real financial details.
- Prevent unauthorized recurring charges.
- If the database is breached, the virtual card can be disabled without affecting your primary account.

### Email Masking for Anonymous Communication

Email masking services create unique, disposable email addresses that forward messages to your real inbox. This protects your primary email from spam and prevents organizations from tracking you across platforms.

**Recommended Services**:

- [SimpleLogin](https://simplelogin.io): Create unlimited aliases and forward them to your real inbox.
- [AnonAddy](https://anonaddy.com): Offers open-source email aliases with advanced privacy settings.
- [ProtonMail (Hide My Email)](https://www.proton.me): For Proton users, integrates email masking with encrypted email services.

**How to Use Email Masking**:

1. Create an alias for the organization you want to donate to.
2. Use the alias when signing up or making a payment on the donation platform.
3. Monitor the alias for communication and disable it if necessary.

**Why Email Masking Helps**:

- Prevent your real email address from being exposed in future data leaks.
- Minimize spam and targeted marketing.
- If an organization's database is compromised, your primary email remains secure.

### Cryptocurrency Donations

Cryptocurrencies like Monero and Bitcoin can be used for anonymous donations.

**Recommended Cryptocurrencies**:

- **Monero (XMR)**: Offers the highest level of anonymity.
- **Bitcoin (BTC)**: Less private but can be anonymized using a mixing service.

**How to Donate with Cryptocurrencies**:

1. Check if the organization accepts cryptocurrency donations.
2. Use a privacy wallet like [Monero Wallet](https://www.getmonero.org) or [Electrum](https://electrum.org).
3. If using Bitcoin, consider a mixing service to obfuscate the transaction.

**Why Cryptocurrencies Help**:

- They don't require personal information.
- Transactions can be anonymized to protect your identity.
- Ideal for organizations that prioritize privacy-friendly payment methods.

### VPN and Browser Privacy

When accessing donation platforms, use a VPN to mask your IP address and ensure your browsing activity remains private.

**Steps for Privacy-Protecting Browsing**:

1. Use a reputable VPN service like [Mullvad](https://mullvad.net) or [ProtonVPN](https://protonvpn.com).
2. Use a privacy-respecting browser like [Brave](https://brave.com) or [Firefox](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/).
3. Consider using the Tor browser for maximum anonymity.

**Why VPNs and Private Browsers Help**:

- Hide your IP address and location from donation platforms.
- Prevent tracking cookies from collecting data about your activities.
- Reduce the risk of third-party surveillance.

## Isolating Your Data

1. **Separate Payment and Communication Details**:
   - Use different virtual cards and masked emails for each organization.
   - Avoid linking donations to accounts with identifiable details (e.g., social media profiles).

2. **Be Cautious of Public Donation Lists**:
   - Some organizations may publicly list donor names or amounts. Check their privacy policy or donation form for acknowledgment options.
   - Use an alias or anonymous donation option if available.

3. **Secure Donation Receipts**:
   - Ensure receipts are sent to your masked email.
   - Store digital copies securely in an encrypted folder or secure cloud storage.

4. **Plan for Future Risks**:
   - Assume databases can be breached in the future. Use tools like Have I Been Pwned to monitor if your information has been compromised.
   - Rotate virtual cards and masked emails periodically for added security.

## Privacy-Preserving Checklist for Donations

Follow these steps to ensure your privacy is protected when donating:

1. **Payment Privacy**:
   - Use a virtual card to process the donation.
   - Consider cryptocurrencies for enhanced anonymity.

2. **Email Privacy**:
   - Use a masked email address to sign up or communicate with the organization.

3. **Connection Security**:
   - Enable a VPN when accessing the donation platform.
   - Use a secure, privacy-focused browser.

4. **Minimize Identifiable Information**:
   - Avoid providing unnecessary personal details like your phone number or physical address.
   - If public acknowledgment is required, use an alias.
